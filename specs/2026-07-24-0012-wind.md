# 0012 — Wind (vent)

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-24
- **Authors:** Hadrien Mary (+ Claude)
- **Relates to:** [0008 tides](2026-07-05-0008-tides.md) (the per-place, shared-across-buoys
  pattern this mirrors — station is to wind what port is to tide), [0002 data dictionary](2026-06-27-0002-data-dictionary.md)
  (extends the canonical schema with a wind namespace), [0005 multi-buoy](2026-06-27-0005-multi-buoy.md).

> Add **wind (vent)** to each buoy from the nearest coastal weather station: mean wind,
> direction and gusts, plus air temperature, rain, humidity and pressure. The point is the
> pairing the buoy can't give on its own — **wind direction × swell direction = offshore /
> onshore**, the single most-asked question for surfing this coast — with air-vs-sea
> temperature and "is it raining" as bonuses.

---

## 1. Why

The buoy answers "how big, from where, how warm (sea)". It says nothing about wind, yet on a
beach coast the wind makes or breaks a session: cross-referencing the station's wind
direction with the buoy's `peak_direction_deg` tells you offshore (clean) vs onshore
(blown-out) at a glance. Air temperature complements the buoy's sea temperature; rain is a
plain "should I go" signal. This is observed weather (a real anemometer), **not a forecast** —
consistent with Foundation §1's no-forecasting stance (astronomical tides were the one
deliberate exception; a forecast wind layer, if ever added, would be a separate decision).

## 2. Decisions

1. **Source = Météo-France open data — Licence Ouverte 2.0 (Etalab).** Commercial reuse and
   redistribution are allowed; attribution "Météo-France" is required. Two products, one per
   phase:
   - **Historical, HOURLY — no key.** The open bulk files on
     [meteo.data.gouv.fr](https://meteo.data.gouv.fr) (`donnees-climatologiques-de-base-horaires`),
     one gzip CSV per department per period. Deep, quality-controlled history (Socoa & Biarritz
     ~100 % hourly coverage from 2011; files reach back to 1980). Columns include `FF`/`DD`
     (mean wind), `FXI`/`DXI` (max instantaneous **gust**), `T`, `RR1`, `U`, `PMER`. **This is
     Phase 1** (this spec's implementation).
   - **Live, 6-MINUTE — needs a key.** The **DPObs "Données d'observation"** API on
     [portail-api.meteofrance.fr](https://portail-api.meteofrance.fr) serves the same stations
     at a 6-minute step, real-time to **H−2 h**, over a short rolling window. Free account,
     **100 req/min**. Key = `METEOFRANCE_API_KEY`, **ingest-only** (never in the client, like
     `API_MAREE_KEY`). **This is Phase 2 (implemented).** Auth = the **`apikey` header** (the
     swagger declares OAuth2 implicit, but the portal accepts the application key directly);
     `GET /public/DPObs/v2/station/infrahoraire-6m?id_station=&date=&format=csv`, one obs per
     call, **units K/Pa** (converted on ingest). **Retry 429s** (don't swallow — LEARNINGS 2026-07-24).
   - *Rejected:* **Windguru** (its station API needs the balise owner's credentials; it's a
     redistributor, not the source). **Open-Meteo** (excellent and keyless, but it serves
     *models* — reanalysis/forecast — and for deep history its grid snaps ~20 km offshore;
     keep it in reserve as a possible future *forecast* layer, not the observed source).

2. **Keyed by STATION, not buoy** — a wind observation is a property of a place, and buoys
   can share a station (exactly like tides/ports, [0008 §8.2]). Each buoy resolves to its
   **nearest curated station within `WIND_MAX_KM = 25`** (`schema.resolve_wind_station`);
   beyond that it has no wind → webapp empty-state. Current resolution:

   | Buoy | Station (`NUM_POSTE`) | Dist. | Character |
   |------|-----------------------|-------|-----------|
   | 06403 Saint-Jean-de-Luz | **Socoa** (64189001) | 1.6 km | coastal semaphore, 21 m |
   | 06402 Anglet | **Biarritz-Pays-Basque** (64024001) | 9.5 km | airport plateau, 71 m |
   | 03302 Cap Ferret | **Cap-Ferret** (33236002) | 15.6 km | coastal, 8 m |

3. **Canonical variables = 8** (owner-selected: "vent + température + pluie + humidité &
   pression"). Units kept as the source emits them (SI-ish, converted for display in the
   webapp later):

   | Canonical | Source | Unit | Notes |
   |-----------|--------|------|-------|
   | `wind_speed_ms` | `FF` | m/s | 10-min mean, headline |
   | `wind_direction_deg` | `DD` | ° | **circular**, headline (offshore/onshore) |
   | `wind_gust_ms` | `FXI` | m/s | max instantaneous gust, headline |
   | `wind_gust_direction_deg` | `DXI` | ° | **circular** |
   | `air_temperature_c` | `T` | °C | complements buoy sea temp |
   | `precipitation_mm` | `RR1` / `rr_per` | mm | **accumulation, not a state** — see [0018](2026-08-24-0018-precipitation-window.md) |
   | `humidity_pct` | `U` | % | **nullable** — absent at Cap-Ferret |
   | `pressure_msl_hpa` | `PMER` | hPa | **nullable** — absent at Cap-Ferret |

   `humidity_pct` and `pressure_msl_hpa` are 100 % present at Socoa & Biarritz but **not
   measured at Cap-Ferret** → first-class missing state, like sea temperature before its
   accumulation start. *Dropped:* hourly min/max temp (`TN`/`TX`, redundant at hourly step),
   radiation/sunshine (airport-only), cloud/visibility (partial, manual). Directions are
   circular (0° = 360°) → circular mean when aggregating, never arithmetic.

   ⚠️ **`precipitation_mm` is the one cumulative variable here, and this table under-specified
   it.** It named only `RR1` (the hourly bulk field) and never covered the 6-min live field
   `rr_per`, whose window is 6 minutes — so one column carried two windows, and the tier builder
   *averaged* a total. Corrected by
   [0018 — Precipitation window & aggregation](2026-08-24-0018-precipitation-window.md): rain
   sums (never means), the bucket is right-closed/left-labelled because both feeds stamp a total
   at the END of its window, and the native tier carries a trailing-hour total.

4. **Storage = one unified series per station, structured exactly like a buoy campaign**
   (spec 0001 §5) under a station-keyed root `wind/<station>/`, symmetric with `tides/<port>/`
   and `buoys/<campaign>/` ([0009]). The buoy-style tiering is the point: the webapp plots a
   station beside a buoy on the **same time axis at any zoom** — years (daily tier) ↔ a single
   day (native/6-min tier). Two feeds fused into one series:
   - `raw/<station>_hist.csv` — the **one-shot hourly history** (2010→seed), immutable (like a
     buoy `*_arch.csv`). Fetched once at seed; **never refreshed**.
   - `raw/<station>_<YEAR>_live.csv` — the **forward-growing 6-min accumulator**, split per year
     (like a `*_reel.csv`). From the seed onward, the record grows *only* from the 6-min feed.
   - `data/{manifest,latest,recent}.json` + `data/year/<station>_<YEAR>.parquet` (native cadence:
     hourly ≤ seed, 6-min after) + `data/hourly/<station>_<YEAR>.parquet` (hourly means) +
     `data/daily.parquet` (daily means) — Snappy, `row_group_size ≈ 1440`, multi-row-group.
   `build_station` coalesces hist+live **hist-preferred**, never interpolating: real gaps — incl.
   the one-time seed-day gap between the last hourly point and the first 6-min point — stay gaps,
   like the buoys. Local mirror: `hfdata/wind/<station>/{raw,data}`.

5. **Manifest.** The station gets its **own** `manifest.json` (station identity + variable dict +
   span + coverage + year/hourly file lists + tiers) — a buoy-shaped manifest so the webapp loads
   a station with the same code path as a buoy. Separately, each *buoy's* `manifest.json` gains a
   `wind` **pointer** block (station id, `num_poste`, label, `distance_km`, `source`) — the analog
   of the `tide` block — so the app knows which station to pair and can name it + attribute it.

## 3. Pipeline (`ingest/wind.py`)

Two feeds, one schema, one tiered dataset — `build_station` mirrors `ingest/build.py`.

- **Seed (one-shot, `--seed`, no key):** resolve the department bulk-file URLs from the data.gouv
  API (survives the `latest-`/`previous-` filename churn), download each gzip (bounded — watchdog
  + transport retry, per the CLAUDE.md "never an unbounded network call in ingest" rule),
  decompress + stream-filter to the station's `NUM_POSTE` (polars all-Utf8 then cast), parse
  `AAAAMMJJHH` as UTC → write `raw/<station>_hist.csv`. Plus an initial 6-min backfill.
- **Refresh (every run, needs the key):** poll the DPObs 6-min grid over a **gap-aware** window
  (§3.1 — one call per point, **429s retried, not swallowed**), convert units
  (K→°C, Pa→hPa) → append to `raw/<station>_<YEAR>_live.csv` (`unique(datetime_utc, keep="last")`).
- **Build:** coalesce hist+live → (re)emit year/hourly/daily + latest/recent + manifest, rewriting
  `data/` from scratch each run so a restructure never orphans a tier. Past-year parquets are
  byte-stable → `sync_bucket` skips them; only the current year + live + JSON move.
- **HF I/O** reuses `update.resolve_token` + `update._net` + `sync_bucket` (pull live always, hist
  only if absent; upload `data/**` + live, and hist on seed). Folded into `update()` next to the
  tide step: **6-min scrape + rebuild every run**; the hourly history is never re-fetched.

A missing/bad station, a missing key, or a fetch failure is **non-fatal**: log and keep what's
there, so one station's blip never aborts the rest.

### 3.1 The live window is gap-aware, not "the last N minutes" (2026-09-03)

**The problem.** The original refresh asked DPObs for a fixed `LIVE_LOOKBACK_MIN = 66` min of grid
points every run. That is fine while the cron runs, and silently lossy the moment it doesn't: on
**2026-09-02** an HF outage failed every refresh from 10:19 to 16:36 UTC, and when it recovered the
**buoy** series had backfilled itself while the **wind** series kept a ~6 h hole (09:36→15:33 UTC,
zero of the 10 points in e.g. hour 06, all 10 of which DPObs was still serving). The asymmetry is in
the feeds, not in our code quality:

| | what the source publishes | what an outage costs |
|---|---|---|
| CANDHIS (buoy) | the **last ~48 h** on every fetch | nothing — the next scrape re-reads the window and coalesces |
| DPObs (wind) | **one observation per call**, addressed by timestamp | everything not asked for; nobody asks twice |

A "last-N-minutes" window can only ever be as robust as the shortest outage it is sized for. Sizing
it to 24 h would just move the cliff.

**The decision.** Derive the request set from **what the accumulator actually holds**
(`wind.live_targets`), not from the clock:

- **Tail** — the last `LIVE_LOOKBACK_MIN` (66 min, 11 points) is re-probed *unconditionally*.
  Points publish a few minutes late, so a slot that answered empty last run may hold data now.
- **Holes** — every 6-min slot of the last `LIVE_HEAL_HOURS` (**24 h**) *missing* from the
  accumulator, **newest first**, capped at `LIVE_HEAL_MAX_POINTS` (**60**, i.e. 6 h) per station
  per run. The cap is the load-bearing part: it keeps one long outage from wedging the 30-min cron
  (CLAUDE.md's "a hang is worse than a crash" rule), and successive runs chew backwards through
  what is left, so a 24 h outage is fully repaired within ~4 runs.
- **Retention** — nothing older than `DPOBS_RETENTION_H` (**96 h**, measured 2026-09-03) is ever
  requested: the API no longer has it. *That* is where a hole becomes permanent, and it is now the
  only place it can.
- **Rate** — a healing run can ask for 71 points × 3 stations, so DPObs calls pass through a
  rolling-window gate (`DPOBS_RATE_PER_MIN = 90`) that keeps us under the documented 100 req/min
  instead of relying on the 429 retry to absorb the burst.

Cost is unchanged when nothing is missing: a healthy run asks for the same 11 points it always did,
because a full heal window contributes **zero** extra calls.

**Operator escape hatch.** `pixi run wind --backfill [HOURS] --all` (default = the full 96 h
retention) lifts the per-run cap for a one-shot repair. `--seed` uses the same path with
`LIVE_SEED_HOURS`.

**Not chosen:** re-downloading the keyless hourly bulk files to patch holes. They would repair a
hole at *hourly* resolution long after DPObs has dropped it, but the history is deliberately a
one-shot immutable seed (§2.4) and a periodic re-fetch of ~50 MB department archives to recover
minutes of data is the wrong trade. Reconsider only if an outage ever outlives the 96 h window.

## 4. Caveats (must surface honestly in the UI, Phase 3)

- **Station ≠ offshore.** Socoa is a coastal semaphore, **Biarritz an airport on a 71 m
  plateau**, Cap-Ferret coastal at 8 m. The wind at the station is *not* the wind at the buoy
  offshore. The UI must **name the station + distance** (exactly like the tide port), never
  present it as "the wind at the buoy". If Biarritz-airport reads poorly for Anglet,
  **Capbreton** (40065002, coastal, 11 m, 16.8 km) is a one-line registry swap.
- **Latency & cadence.** The history is a **one-shot** seed (hourly, through ~J−1 at seed time);
  forward, the series is **6-min to H−2 h**. A one-time gap sits on the seed day between the last
  hourly point and the first 6-min point (a real gap, not interpolated).
- **Depth.** History is pulled from 2010 to match the wave archive; Cap-Ferret may start later
  (fine — it just accumulates forward, like a realtime-only buoy).

  the paired buoy on the same time axis (years ↔ day); the **offshore/onshore** cross-reference
  (station `wind_direction_deg` × buoy `peak_direction_deg`); air-vs-sea temperature; honest
  station attribution (name + distance). Default buoy+station pairs, with a user override for the
  pairing (UX TBD).
