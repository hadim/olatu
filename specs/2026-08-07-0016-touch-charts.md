# 0016 — Touch interaction on the chart stack

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Supersedes / relates:** replaces the touch gesture model of
[0006 §6](2026-06-28-0006-stack-migration-a11y.md) (`lib/uplotTouch.ts`, one-finger pan). The
desktop interactions (drag-zoom, double-click reset, hover card) are unchanged.

## 1. Why

Two owner reports from a phone, both about the same surface:

1. **The chart stack was a scroll trap.** Every panel called `preventDefault()` on *every*
   one-finger `touchmove` in order to pan the x-scale, and no panel set `touch-action`. The page
   therefore could not be scrolled with a finger anywhere over the charts — and the stack is
   ~1700 px tall on a 390 px-wide phone, so it was most of the page.
2. **The reading you asked for was unreadable.** Tapping a panel put the values on screen, but the
   date/time lives in the `.hover-card` **above the whole stack**. Measured at 390 px: the card sits
   at y≈2059 and is 338 px tall; the panels run 2496 → 4155. Touch a lower panel and the card is
   ~1700 px off-screen; touch the first one and the card is under your hand. And the readout
   vanished the moment you lifted your finger — the one moment it becomes visible.

## 2. Decision

### Gesture model

| Gesture | Owner | Action |
|---|---|---|
| One finger, **vertical** | the **browser** | page scrolls (native momentum + rubber-banding) |
| One finger, **horizontal** | us | **scrub** — move the cursor, read values along the series |
| Two fingers, drag | us | pan the x-scale |
| Two fingers, pinch | us | zoom the x-scale about the midpoint |

One finger now **reads** instead of panning. Reading is the thing you reach for on a phone, and it
was the worse-served of the two: it used to be tap-only, one value at a time. Panning loses nothing
— it is still on two fingers, the `‹ ›` buttons, the range chips and the history ribbon.

Two mechanisms cooperate, deliberately:

- **`touch-action: pan-y`** on each plot's hit area tells the browser it owns vertical panning, so
  scrolling is *native* (JS can never match its momentum or rubber-banding) and we get a
  `touchcancel` once it takes over. It also kills double-tap page zoom over the plot, which used to
  fight the cursor.
- **A JS axis-lock** (`AXIS_LOCK_PX = 8`) decides on the first significant move whether the gesture
  is ours (|dx| ≥ |dy|) or the browser's. Belt and braces: engines cancel our touches at different
  moments, and without the lock a slightly-diagonal scroll would jitter the cursor on its way past.
  Below the threshold nothing happens, so a **tap stays a tap**.

### The pinned readout

A compact bar **fixed to the viewport**, not sticky inside the section: anything anchored to the
section scrolls away from the panel you are actually touching. It shows the scrubbed date/time,
appears on the first scrub, and is **deliberately not cleared when you lift your finger** — the
values under a finger are only readable once the finger is gone. Tap the ✕ to dismiss, which also
drops the cursor on every panel (otherwise a persisted cursor could never be put away).

It carries only the timestamp. Making the existing 338 px card sticky would eat ~40 % of a phone
screen for the duration of every gesture; the values themselves are already legible on-plot, in
each panel's cursor bubble.

The default card hint also branches on `(hover: none)`: "Hover the chart" is simply false on a
phone, so a coarse pointer reads `chart_touch_hint` ("Slide a finger across a chart…").

## 3. What this does not change

Desktop: mouse drag-zoom, double-click reset, the hover card and the per-panel cursor bubbles all
behave exactly as before — the bar never appears for a mouse, since it is only raised by a touch
scrub. Panel reorder stays on Pointer Events (0013 §7) and is untouched; so is the `HeatRibbon`
drag band.

## 4. Verified

Synthetic touch sequences at 390 px against a live panel: `touch-action` computes to `pan-y`; a
vertical drag is **not** `preventDefault`ed and raises no bar; a horizontal drag is prevented,
raises the bar at `top: 0`, and the bar's timestamp matches the hover card's; the bar survives
`touchend`; the ✕ hides it and resets the card; a two-finger pinch is still prevented and handled.
